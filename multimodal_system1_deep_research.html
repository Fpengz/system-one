Executive Thesis
The natural temptation after Jev's emergence is to ask: "Can we make Jev see images?" That question is already answered by engineering releases from Cloudflare, Perplexity, InternLM, and open-source groups. The durable scientific challenge is not modality ingestion, but multimodal decision intelligence: building fast, non-autoregressive decision procedures whose uncertainties obey formal probabilistic axioms, maintain robustness across equivalent queries, and dynamically escalate to expensive reasoning models only when counterfactual deliberation produces strictly positive expected net utility.

4.1 The Core Decision-Model Abstraction

Jev’s important contribution is not conventional classification, but a software interface: the caller describes one or more decision fields and their valid outputs, and the model returns typed answers with normalized probabilities [1]. This distinguishes the interface from a traditional classifier with a frozen ontology. DecisionBench formalizes roughly the same abstraction across 43 tasks and 28 domains [11], while the Decision Index documents an expanding ecosystem of open reproductions [12].

4.3 Prior Discriminative Multimodal Scoring Foundations

Even prior to typed-decision systems, multimodal machine learning possessed strong non-generative decision baselines. SigLIP 2 provides state-of-the-art multilingual zero-shot discriminative representations [13]. Garosi et al. demonstrate that large vision-language models can operate as flexible in-context classifiers [14]. Similarly, ImageReward established direct human preference scoring over text-image pairs [21]. Scientific novelty cannot merely reside in "scores instead of text".

5. RQ2: Reliability Is the More Durable Open Problem

1. Marginal Calibration Deficits
A model may display low overall Expected Calibration Error (ECE), yet remain brittle in deployment:
  • Binary decision probabilities skew heavily around default 0.5 thresholds [2].
  • Zero-shot CLIP requires dataset-specific temperature adjustments [15].
  • Semantically equivalent prompt rewrites shift safety classification probabilities by up to 25% [16].
2. Probabilistic Coherence Failures
Probabilities violate fundamental axioms of Kolmogorov probability theory:
  • Complementarity failure: P(A)+P(¬A)≠1.0P(A) + P(\neg A) \neq 1.0P(A)+P(¬A)=1.0 [4].
  • Partition failure: mutually exclusive subsets exceed aggregate probability mass.
  • Ordinal scale compression: models avoid extremes on 5- and 7-point scales [5].

The Four Axiomatic Pathology Failure Modes

1. Negation Incompleteness Axiom I
P(True∣Q)+P(False∣Q)≠1.0P(\text{True}\mid Q) + P(\text{False}\mid Q) \neq 1.0P(True∣Q)+P(False∣Q)=1.0
Li et al. [4] prove that queries and their direct syntactic negations fail complementarity, preventing reliable downstream Boolean aggregation.
2. Scale Compression Axiom II
Var(Pordinal)≪Var(Platent)\mathrm{Var}(P_{\text{ordinal}}) \ll \mathrm{Var}(P_{\text{latent}})Var(Pordinal​)≪Var(Platent​)
Gao et al. [5] show that as rating candidates expand to 5 or 7 options, direct decision models compress probability away from extreme values.
3. Prompt Rewrite Jitter Symmetry
ΔP≤∣P(y∣q)−P(y∣q′)∣≫0\Delta P \le |P(y|q) - P(y|q')| \gg 0ΔP≤∣P(y∣q)−P(y∣q′)∣≫0
Weng et al. [16] demonstrate that paraphrase rewrites create massive score drift in VLM classifiers; ensemble averaging is required to tame variance.
4. Permutation Bias Invariance
P(ci∣C)≠P(ci∣π(C))P(c_i \mid C) \neq P(c_i \mid \pi(C))P(ci​∣C)=P(ci​∣π(C))
Reordering option lists in the candidate prompt systematically skews decision likelihood, violating the Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives.

The 7 Requirements for a True Multimodal Decision Benchmark

DecisionBench [11] and Decision Index [12] establish text protocols. An impactful multimodal benchmark cannot merely wrap existing VQA datasets under a Jev API. It must systematically audit:

1. Dynamic runtime candidate set variations (CCC)
2. Image, video frame, and audio paired state inputs
3. Strict ECE and adaptive binning calibration metrics
4. Logical negation & partition consistency audits
5. Explicit abstention & Out-of-Distribution testing
6. Latency, FLOPs, and memory normalized efficiency
7. Paraphrase and candidate permutation stress-testing

6. Fast/Slow Multimodal Inference: The Adjacent Frontier

Recent vision-language research already explores adaptive computation, but primarily within homogenous autoregressive pipelines:

Yu et al. (2024) [17]
Distilling S2 into S1: Compiles multi-step reasoning traces into amortized model weights, preserving accuracy while eliminating deliberation overhead.
FAST (2025) [18]
Adaptive Reasoning Length: Dynamically modulates visual CoT token budgets, achieving major compute savings on standard benchmarks.
SCAN (AAAI 2026) [19]
Coarse-to-Fine S1/S2: Uses fast candidate identification and only invokes granular visual reasoning when coarse confidence is low.
CritiqueDrive (2026) [20]
Latent Distillation: Verifier-guided RL teacher distills latent representations into a fast driving student, dramatically lowering inference latency.
⚡
The Typed Advantage in Fast/Slow Cascades
Unlike open-ended text generators, a typed multimodal decision model possesses three structural traits that make fast/slow integration mathematically tractable:
  1. Native Confidence Signal: Emits normalized distribution P(Y∣S,Q,C)P(Y \mid S, Q, C)P(Y∣S,Q,C), giving an immediate routing indicator.
  2. Bounded Search Space: The slow model is restricted to evaluating explicit candidate set CCC, avoiding open-ended degenerative sampling.
  3. Quantifiable Utility Function: The downstream software defines explicit risk tolerance λ\lambdaλ, making test-time compute allocation directly optimizable.

9. Risks and Falsification Criteria

To maintain rigorous falsifiability, the research design establishes concrete kill-criteria:

Risk 1: High Architecture
Generic VLM Option Scoring Suffices
If a frozen VLM backbone with simple token scoring matches specialized decision models on calibration, latency, and robustness, the specialized architecture is unjustified [3].
Risk 2: Critical Routing
Confidence Fails to Predict S2 Gain
A cascade collapses if S1S_1S1​ uncertainty cannot identify instances where S2S_2S2​ actually corrects errors. If gain is uncorrelated with uncertainty, always-fast or always-slow dominates.
Risk 3: High Correlation
S1 & S2 Errors Correlate
If the reasoning model fails on the exact same ambiguous visual cases where the fast model fails, deliberation provides zero excess utility.
Risk 4: High Distribution
Calibration Collapses Under Shift
In-distribution calibration is insufficient for autonomous software. If calibration error spikes on new camera angles or domains, safety guarantees dissolve.
Risk 5: Medium Systems
Encoding Dominates Latency
If heavy visual prefill consumes 95% of inference time, optimizing the decision head yields negligible end-to-end savings. Requires visual state cache amortization.
Methodology
Falsification Protocol
Each experiment tracks whether learned Δ(S)\Delta(S)Δ(S) beats a naïve compute-matched baseline. Failure to achieve ≥15%\ge 15\%≥15% Pareto frontier displacement triggers early program pivot.

10. Conclusion & Strategic North Star

RQ1 Verdict
Multimodality in typed models is an engineered reality, not an open question. Commercial releases already ingest images and video. Pure modality expansion is unpublishable.
RQ2 Verdict
Reliability is deeply compromised: probabilities fail elementary Kolmogorov axioms, ordinal scales compress, and prompt rewrites introduce severe variance.
RQ3 Verdict
The winning research formulation is a calibrated, coherent multimodal System-1 model paired with selective System-2 escalation, evaluated along an explicit risk-compute Pareto frontier.
“The strategic lesson is to make the research question invariant to the current product wave. 'Can a model see images?' is obsolete in weeks. 'Can a machine make fast multimodal decisions whose uncertainty is semantically coherent and knows when deliberation is worth the compute?' is a durable foundation for modern artificial intelligence.”
